Age & stage guide

What to play at every stage

Every child develops at their own pace, so use this as a guide. The best toy is the one your child gets absorbed in, with a grown-up joining in.

Ages 1–2

Discovering the senses

  • Cloth books, xylophones, different textures
  • Posting, banging and grasping
  • Simple cause-and-effect toys

⚠️ Avoid small parts and check age warnings on the packaging.

Ages 3–5

Matching and pretending

  • Mazes, screws and peg games
  • Colour and shape matching, counting
  • Play kitchens and shopkeeping

💡 Cooperative games are a better start than competitive ones.

Ages 6–8

Science and building

  • Microscopes, telescopes, light experiments
  • Wooden models, magnetic tiles, mechanisms
  • Strategy and maths games

📚 Match toys to primary science topics.

Ages 9+

Bigger projects

  • Hydraulic and solar kits
  • Digital microscopes, stargazing missions
  • Deduction and cooperative strategy games

🏆 Encourage them to film builds or keep an observation journal.

Buying tips

Before you buy,
ask 4 questions

More toys doesn't mean better play. Here's what the Kidrise team has learned from years of choosing toys and learning tools for families and schools.

Buying a gift? Try the gift finder →

Will a grown-up play too?

15 minutes of play together teaches more than an hour of playing alone.

Can it be played more than one way?

Open-ended toys grow with your child and get played with for longer.

Is it age-appropriate and safe?

Check the age label and small-parts warnings, especially for under-3s.

Do you have space for it?

Put out 2–3 toys at a time and rotate the rest. Children value and focus on them more.
