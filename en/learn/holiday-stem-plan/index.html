Holiday STEM plan at home:
4 weekly themes + 30-day challenge

Stuck for ideas in the summer, Christmas or Easter holidays? A 4-week STEM plan (light, forces, living things, chemistry), 15–30 minutes a day, with a printable 30-day challenge.

Looking for more family activity ideas?

What shall we do today?

The line you hear most in a long holiday: “I'm bored, what can I do?” Rather than thinking of something new every day, plan a theme in advance. One science theme a week and 15–30 minutes a day gives children something to look forward to, and saves parents a daily headache.

How to use it: pick a theme each week, do a small activity Monday to Friday, a “big challenge” on Saturday, and rest or explore outside on Sunday. Track everything on the 30-day STEM challenge chart and colour in each day you finish.

The 4-week plan

Week 1☀️ Light and shadowShadow Zoo, a water glass rainbow, a torch treasure hunt, measuring shadowsMake a cardboard periscope
Week 2🌉 Forces and structuresPaper bridge challenge, one-minute tower, balancing bird, lolly stick catapultBuild the tallest tower from 20 sheets of paper
Week 3🌱 Living things and natureSeed sprouting diary, leaf collecting, magnifier world, bug watchingComplete nature bingo in the park
Week 4🧪 Materials and changeKitchen volcano, sink or float, dancing raisins, sugar water rainbowRed cabbage acid and alkali test

A 20-minute daily rhythm

  1. 3 minutes: ask a question, such as “How can we make the paper bridge hold more coins?”
  2. 12 minutes: do it, letting your child lead while you help only when needed.
  3. 5 minutes: record and share: draw the result and finish the sentence “I found out that…”.

Adjusting for age

  • Ages 3–5: focus on doing and describing, such as “Which shadow is bigger?” No writing needed.
  • Ages 6–8: add predictions and simple measuring, like measuring shadows with a ruler or counting coins.
  • Ages 9–12: add fair tests and data tables. Change one variable and compare the results.

Tips to keep the plan going

  • Put the materials on a tray the night before, so they're ready in the morning.
  • Let your child choose the order of the day's activities so they feel in charge.
  • After each week, take a photo of everything you made and stick it on the fridge.
  • Invite grandparents or friends to join the weekend challenge.
  • Don't aim for perfect. Skipping a day is fine; the point is to have fun.

FAQ

How can working parents fit this in?

Move weekday activities to 15 minutes after dinner, or let a carer follow the activity cards. Save the weekend challenge to do together.

Do I need to buy lots of materials?

Most activities only use everyday things like paper, tape, cups, a torch and kitchen supplies. To go further, add a microscope, telescope or wooden model.

Can siblings with a big age gap play together?

Yes. In the same activity, the older child records and measures while the younger one does and observes, and they finish it together.
