Stuck at home on a rainy day?
12 screen-free family activities

Hong Kong's rainy season is long. When Red or Black Rainstorm warnings keep you in, what else can kids do besides watch TV? 12 activities by age using things at home, plus a free rainy day bingo.

Looking for more family activity ideas?

What shall we do today?

Hong Kong's rainy season is long. When a Red or Black Rainstorm warning or a typhoon keeps children indoors all day, it easily becomes a “TV day”. With a ready-made “rainy day box”, though, the living room and kitchen turn into a lab and a playground. These 12 activities all use common household things and are grouped by age.

Make a “rainy day box”: a torch, paper cups, tape, rubber bands, paper clips, baking soda, white vinegar, food colouring, coloured paper and dice. When the rain comes, it's ready to go.

Ages 1–3: senses and fine motor

  1. DIY sensory bag: glitter flows inside a sealed bag and changes with every press, with no mess.
  2. Sound bottle matching: fill bottles with rice, beans and buttons to train listening.
  3. Clothes peg challenge: clip pegs around a paper plate to build finger strength.

Ages 3–5: pretend play and matching

  1. Home supermarket: shop from a list and pay, practising counting and conversation.
  2. Shadow zoo: lights off, torch on, and there's a zoo on the wall.
  3. Home number hunt: find numbers on clocks, remote controls and door plates.

Ages 5–8: experiments and challenges

  1. Kitchen volcano: baking soda plus vinegar and the bubbles come pouring out.
  2. One-minute tower: 60 seconds to build the tallest tower from paper cups.
  3. Living room treasure map: draw a map and hide treasure to practise spatial thinking.
  4. Story dice: roll for characters and places, then tell a story together.

Ages 8+: engineering and science

  1. Paper bridge challenge: how many coins can one sheet of A4 hold?
  2. Lolly stick catapult: build a catapult and compete on distance and accuracy.

For the whole family: rainy day home bingo

Siblings of different ages playing together? Download the rainy day home bingo: 16 indoor missions where older children help younger ones and finish it together.

Tips for a smoother rainy day

  • Plan ahead: when the Observatory issues a rainstorm or typhoon warning, choose 2–3 activities. Use the weekly family activity planner.
  • Break it up: keep each activity to 15–30 minutes, with time to move in between (like indoor hopscotch).
  • Tidy up together: tidying is part of the activity and builds responsibility.
  • Allow a little mess: lay old newspaper or a plastic tablecloth on the floor so everyone can relax.

FAQ

Won't kids be bored without any screens?

An adult may need to get things started, but as long as there's a challenge or a surprise (like a volcano eruption or a timed race), children usually get absorbed quickly.

What if we don't have all the materials?

Most activities have alternatives: blocks instead of paper cups, a phone torch instead of a torch. Each activity page lists what you need.
