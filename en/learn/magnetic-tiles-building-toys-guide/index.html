Magnetic tiles, blocks or wooden models?
A guide to building toys

Building toys grow spatial sense, engineering thinking and patience. We compare magnetic tiles, interlocking pieces, screw-together kits and wooden models by age, plus magnet safety.

What shall we do today?

Building toys are the longest-lasting kind of STEM toy: with the same box of magnetic tiles, a 3-year-old makes flat shapes, a 6-year-old builds a 3D house and a 9-year-old tests how much a structure can hold. Choose the right type and one box can last for years.

In short: start ages 3–5 with large magnetic tiles or interlocking pieces; add screw-together kits at 5–8 to practise using tools; from 8, try wooden models with real mechanisms such as hydraulics and levers.

Four kinds of building toy compared

TypeAgesStrengthsWatch out for
Magnetic tiles3+Easy to build and take apart, very 3D, quick successMagnets must be fully sealed
Interlocking pieces2–6Builds fine motor skills and force controlChoose the right size for age and avoid small pieces
Screw-together kits4–8Introduces tools, gears and step-by-step assemblyLots of parts, so you'll need a storage box
Wooden / mechanical models7–12Building from diagrams, understanding mechanismsTakes patience; an adult helps

What do building toys develop?

  • Spatial sense: imagining flat shapes in 3D, an important foundation for geometry and later science.
  • Engineering thinking: when it falls, find out why, change the design and try again. That's the design cycle.
  • Following steps: building from instructions trains reading diagrams and carrying out multi-step tasks.
  • Creativity and expression: in free building, children tell stories and design characters and scenes.

From following instructions to free creation

Many parents worry their child will build the instructions once and never play again. Try guiding them through three stages:

  1. Build from the diagram: finish one example to build confidence.
  2. Change one thing: “Can you add another storey?” “Try a different roof?”
  3. Set a challenge: give a goal instead of a diagram, such as “build a bridge that can hold a toy car”. Use the building challenge cards and draw one each time.

Magnet toy safety

⚠️ Important: if a child swallows two or more strong magnets, they can attract each other inside the gut and cause serious injury. When buying and using magnetic toys:
  • Magnets must be fully enclosed in plastic and unable to come loose.
  • Check regularly for cracked tiles and stop using any with cracks.
  • With under-3s at home, put the tiles away after play.
  • If you suspect your child has swallowed a magnet, seek medical help immediately. Don't wait for symptoms.

Storage tips

Scattered pieces are the biggest headache with building toys. Use a clear storage box with compartments and sort by colour or shape. Lay down a tray or mat before playing so you can tip everything straight back into the box. Letting your child do the last step of tidying up is great practice in responsibility.

FAQ

How many magnetic tiles do we need?

For one child, 60–100 pieces is enough for 3D houses, towers and cars. For siblings or large builds, consider adding more.

What age are wooden models for?

Generally 7 and up, depending on the number of parts and difficulty. For the first one, build it together, with the adult doing the fiddlier steps.

What if my child gets frustrated halfway through?

Take a break, then break the task into smaller pieces: “Today we'll just get to step 5.” The success of finishing a small goal matters more than finishing it all at once.
