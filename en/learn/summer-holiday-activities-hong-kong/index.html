Summer holidays in Hong Kong:
an 8-week family plan

Looking for more family activity ideas?

What shall we do today?

Hong Kong's summer holiday lasts about two months. In week one children are excited; by week three they're bored; by late August it's screens all day. A simple plan set up before the holiday (one theme a week, one small activity a day, one outing a week) makes the summer full without parents having to invent something new every morning.

The summer framework: one theme a week → one 15–30 minute activity each weekday → one related outing at the weekend → track it on the Weekly Family Activity Planner. Children who help choose the themes join in far more.

Where to go in Hong Kong: indoors and out

🔬 Museums

The Hong Kong Science Museum and Space Museum have plenty of hands-on exhibits for ages 5 and up. Some museums close one fixed day a week, so check the official website before you go.

🌿 Country parks and wetlands

Family walks are short and fairly flat, good for little legs. Summer is hot, so set off early and bring plenty of water. More in our Hong Kong nature spots guide.

🏖️ Beaches and pools

Only swim during lifeguard hours and inside the marked area, and check beach flags and water quality.

📚 Public libraries

Air-conditioned and free, and many branches run family reading events in summer. Pair it with the Family Reading Log.

Back-up plans for heat, rainstorms and typhoons

Hong Kong summers bring frequent heat warnings, rainstorms and typhoons. Have a back-up ready so plans don't fall apart:

  • Very hot weather: skip outdoor play around midday and try Ice Dino Rescue or a sink-or-float test indoors instead.
  • Rainstorms and typhoons: stay home and play Rainy Day Home Bingo; more ideas in rainy day activities in Hong Kong.
  • Thunderstorms: while a thunderstorm warning is in force, avoid open areas, beaches and hillsides.

Summer screen time: agree the rules first

Screen time is the easiest thing to lose control of over summer. On day one of the holiday, set up a Family Screen Time Agreement with your child: how long each day, when screens are allowed, and what needs to be done first. Rules children help write are much easier to keep. More in our screen time guide.

Toys that last the whole summer

If you're adding a toy or two for the holiday, choose ones that fit several themes and get played with again and again: binoculars for birdwatching on hikes; a microscope for leaves and insects at home; magnetic tiles and marble mazes for rainy indoor days; and a screw-together robot car for older children who want an engineering challenge.

FAQ

Do we need an activity every day?

No need to fill every hour. One 15–30 minute theme activity a day, plus free play and rest, is plenty. A bit of boredom actually helps creativity.

We both work. How can we follow a summer plan?

Hand weekday activities to a carer, or let older children do them on their own, such as an observation diary or reading log. Spend 10 minutes in the evening hearing what they did, and do the outing together at the weekend.

What should we watch out for on summer outings?

Check the Observatory's weather warnings and UV index, avoid outdoor activities in the midday heat, bring water, hats and sun protection, and check opening hours before you go.
