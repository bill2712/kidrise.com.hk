Lunar New Year with kids:
10 activities + gift ideas

What to do over Lunar New Year 2027 (Year of the Goat)? 10 family activities built on Hong Kong traditions (fai chun, lai see money skills, narcissus diary, candy box maths) plus gift ideas for family gatherings.

What shall we do today?

Lunar New Year's Day 2027, the start of the Year of the Goat, falls on 6 February. For many children the holiday means visiting relatives, collecting lai see and eating sweets. But fai chun, New Year flowers, the candy box and lai see all hide something to play with and learn from. These 10 activities run from the big clean-up before New Year to the third day of the holiday.

3 simple rules: ① let children take part in the traditions, not just watch; ② 15–30 minutes per activity is plenty around visiting; ③ ask “why?” to help children understand what each custom means.

Before New Year: getting ready

  1. The big New Year clean-up: break chores into small task cards, such as wiping window frames, tidying toys or folding lai see envelopes. Track them on the Weekly Little Helper Chore Chart with a sticker for each one done.
  2. Narcissus or New Year flower diary: buy a narcissus bulb or a kumquat plant and measure the stem and count the buds every day in the 14-Day Plant Growth Diary. Will the narcissus bloom right on New Year's Day?
  3. Write your own fai chun: get red paper and a brush (or a thick marker). Younger children can write 福 (fortune) or draw a goat; older ones can try a four-character greeting and learn why 福 is often hung upside down.

New Year's Eve and Day: reunion and visiting

  1. Candy box maths: how many sweets are in each compartment? How many altogether? Sort them by colour or shape, then make a “most popular sweet” chart. Great for ages 4–8 to practise counting, sorting and simple data.
  2. Greeting practice: role-play before you head out. Practise New Year greetings, receiving lai see with both hands and saying thank you. Shy children feel much more confident after a rehearsal at home.
  3. New Year story chain: use Story Dice and add New Year elements like the Nian monster, firecrackers and the colour red, then take turns telling a brand-new Nian story.

Days 2 and 3: play and learn at home

  1. Lai see money lesson: count the lai see money together and split it into three envelopes: save, spend and share. Then play Home Supermarket to practise paying, giving change and comparing prices.
  2. The science of red: why is everything red at New Year? Try Colour Mixing Magic and see what red makes with yellow, or with blue.
  3. Family board game tournament: at family gatherings, board games let children of different ages play together. Keep score on the Family Game Night Scorecard and crown a “New Year Game Champion”.
  4. New Year kindness: give fai chun to neighbours, write cards to grandparents, sort old toys to donate. Keep the good wishes going with the 21-Day Kindness Calendar.

Lunar New Year gift ideas for kids

Besides lai see, many parents prepare a small gift for their own or relatives' children. Unlike Christmas, a New Year gift works best when it's something the family can play together at gatherings, and it should avoid unlucky associations.

OccasionWhat to look forExamples
Visiting relatives (ages 3–5)Simple rules, everything in one boxDon't Bother Jerry cheese game, carrot harvest game, NEOLAND colour code game
Big family gatherings (4+)Many players, about 15 minutes a roundHape Pallina, wooden family tumbling tower
Primary-age children (6–12)Strategy, maths, years of playPrime Climb maths board game
  • Good meaning: board games suggest “the whole family together”, which suits New Year well.
  • Avoid taboos: clocks and watches are traditionally avoided as New Year gifts.
  • Order early: couriers and shops change their hours around Lunar New Year, so plan one to two weeks ahead.

FAQ

The holiday is short and full of visits. How do we fit activities in?

One or two 15-minute activities a day is enough: practise greetings before you leave, count lai see when you get home. Bring a board game to relatives' homes so children have something to do while adults chat.

Should children manage their own lai see money?

Hand over control step by step. Kindergarten children can start by counting and sorting; primary children can decide how to use the “spend” envelope and track what they spend, learning to plan and choose.

How do I choose a board game for a relative's child?

Go by the age on the box and pick a game with simple rules and short rounds of about 15 minutes. If you're unsure of the child's age or interests, a cooperative or hand-eye game for ages 3–8 that adults can join is a safe choice.
