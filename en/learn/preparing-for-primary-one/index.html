Getting ready for Primary One:
an 8-point checklist for parents

Moving from kindergarten to primary school means longer lessons, more self-care and new ways of learning. 8 skills to build at home before P1, a morning routine chart and summer practice ideas.

What shall we do today?

Starting Primary One is a big step: longer lessons, copying homework into a handbook, packing their own school bag, and buying snacks or going to the toilet on their own at recess. More than drilling dictation early, self-care, a steady routine and focus usually decide how well children settle in.

In short: from the second term of K3, practise one small skill at a time. The goal is for your child to start school thinking, “I can do this!”

The 8-point checklist

  • A steady routine: 4–6 weeks before term, shift wake-up to school time and keep a fixed bedtime.
  • A morning routine: get up, wash, brush teeth, put on uniform, eat breakfast. Let your child tick each step on a morning routine chart.
  • Self-care: doing up buttons and zips, tying or fastening shoes, opening a water bottle, tidying clothes after the toilet.
  • Packing a school bag: pack books and homework from the timetable, practising “check the list → pack → tick”.
  • Focus: start with 10-minute quiet activities and build up to 20–30 minutes.
  • Fine motor skills and pencil grip: play with pegs, mazes and scissors to prepare for copying work (see our fine motor skills article).
  • Asking for help: practise saying to a teacher “May I go to the toilet?” and “I don't understand, could you say it again?”
  • Handling feelings: use words for nerves and sadness, and know who to ask for help.

Focus: games instead of drills

Many parents worry their child can't sit still. Rather than making them sit through worksheets, use fun activities to gradually stretch their attention span:

Board games

Waiting, taking turns and following rules make them the most natural focus practice.

Jigsaws and mazes

A clear goal and a feeling of success at the end.

Reading together

15 minutes each night trains listening and understanding (try the family reading log).

Timed challenges

Use a sand timer or timer: “Let's build for 10 minutes, then have a break.”

Chores are the best self-care training

Setting the table, pairing socks, watering plants, putting dirty clothes in the basket: simple chores build responsibility, hand skills and finishing tasks step by step. Use the weekly little helper chart and pick 2–3 regular jobs each week.

The first month of school: what parents can do

  1. Listen first after school: specific questions like “Who did you play with at recess?” get better answers than “How was your day?”
  2. Keep time for play: as well as homework, children still need free play and rest every day.
  3. Keep in touch with the teacher: check the handbook and notices, and contact the class teacher early with any concerns.
  4. Accept the adjustment period: tiredness and ups and downs in the first few weeks are common, so give your child extra patience.

FAQ

Should my child learn to write lots of characters before P1?

There's no need for early drilling. A good pencil grip, fine motor skills and an interest in learning matter more than writing lots of characters early.

My child is scared of new places. How can I help them settle?

Visit the school or walk nearby beforehand, look at photos of the campus together and talk through a primary school day, so the unknown feels less worrying.

When should we start adjusting the routine?

Start 4–6 weeks before term, moving wake-up and bedtime 15 minutes earlier each week. It's much easier than a sudden change the night before school starts.
