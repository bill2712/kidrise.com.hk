The new primary science subject:
how parents can help at home

Primary Science is being introduced in Hong Kong in phases from 2025/26, with a focus on hands-on inquiry. The basic inquiry skills, 7 things parents can do at home, and free experiment logs and activities.

What shall we do today?

According to the Education Bureau, the old primary General Studies subject is being restructured, and the new Primary Science subject starts in P1 and P4 from the 2025/26 school year before extending to other year levels. It puts more weight on hands-on inquiry, which is good news for parents: playing, asking and testing things at home with your child is the best preparation there is.

For implementation details and curriculum content, please refer to the latest information from the Education Bureau and your school.

What does “scientific inquiry” actually involve?

Rather than memorising facts, science lessons aim to teach children to “think like a scientist”. These skills can all be practised at home:

Inquiry skillHow to practise at home
ObservingLook at leaves with a magnifier, record the moon's shape
Asking questionsEncourage your child to say their “whys” out loud, and write them down together
PredictingBefore an experiment, ask “What do you think will happen?”
Fair testingChange one thing at a time, such as only the wings of a paper plane
Measuring and recordingUse a ruler, timer and thermometer, and turn results into a table
CommunicatingExplain what they found to the family afterwards

7 things parents can do at home

1. Turn “why?” into a mini experiment

When your child asks “Why does ice melt?”, instead of answering straight away, say “Shall we test it?” Ice Dino Rescue is a great start.

2. Build a habit with a record sheet

Use the same science experiment log every time: question, prediction, steps, observations, conclusion. Gradually, your child will start thinking in this framework naturally.

3. Let them predict wrong

Wrong predictions are when children learn most. Asking “Why was it different from what I thought?” is more valuable than getting it right.

4. Go outside

Hong Kong's parks, beaches and countryside are the best science classrooms. Take a park nature bingo sheet to observe plants, animals and the weather.

5. Try long-term observation

Growing bean sprouts, recording the weather or drawing the moon's phases over one or two weeks builds patience and an eye for data.

6. Visit science museums

The Hong Kong Science Museum and Hong Kong Space Museum have plenty of interactive exhibits. Afterwards, let your child pick an exhibit and try a related mini experiment at home.

7. Choose the right tools

A magnifier or microscope, a pair of binoculars, a tape measure and a timer are enough for most primary-level investigations.

FAQ

Does my child need extra tutoring for primary science?

The subject focuses on the process of inquiry, so everyday hands-on activities, observing and asking questions often help more than memorising notes. If needed, find out how your school teaches and assesses the subject first.

We don't have space for experiments at home. What can we do?

Most primary-level experiments only need a kitchen counter and a tray. Our family activities can be filtered by “indoors”, and they mainly use everyday household items.

My younger child can't write much yet. How can they record results?

They can draw, use stickers, or tell you what they saw while you write it down. What matters is that they express their own observations.
