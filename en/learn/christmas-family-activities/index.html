Christmas family activities:
12 STEM ideas at home

What to do over the Christmas holidays? 12 Christmas activities using things you have at home: paper snowflake symmetry, ice melting races, a cup Christmas tree tower and a light-up card, plus a free advent calendar.

Looking for more family activity ideas?

What shall we do today?

The best part of Christmas often isn't unwrapping presents but the time spent making things together. These 12 Christmas activities use everyday materials and turn snowflakes, Christmas trees and fairy lights into small science experiments for ages 3 to 12.

Want one a day? Download the free Christmas STEM Advent Calendar: a 10-minute challenge every day from 1 December to Christmas.

Ages 3–5: senses and crafts

  1. Ice rescue: freeze small toys in ice and “rescue” them with warm water, salt and spoons. See Ice Dino Rescue, and talk about which method melts ice fastest.
  2. Jingle bell bottles: fill bottles with rice, beans and bells, then guess which one is the “jingle bell”. See Sound Bottle Matching.
  3. Red and green sort: hunt for red and green things around the home, sort them and count which colour wins, practising sorting and counting.
  4. Christmas story dice: use Story Dice with Santa, reindeer and snowmen as extra characters, and take turns telling the story.

Ages 5–8: Christmas science experiments

  1. Paper snowflake symmetry: fold a square into triangles, cut, and open it up to reveal a symmetrical snowflake. Pair it with the Mirror Symmetry Drawing worksheet.
  2. One-minute Christmas tree: build the tallest paper-cup “Christmas tree” in 60 seconds, as in the One-Minute Tower. How many cups on the bottom row make it most stable?
  3. Dancing Christmas elves: drop raisins into a fizzy drink and watch them bob up and down, as in Dancing Raisins, to explore bubbles and buoyancy.
  4. A light-up Christmas card: use an LED, a coin cell battery and copper tape to make a star that glows. Plan the circuit first on the Simple Circuit Planner. (Adult supervision needed; keep button batteries away from young children.)

Ages 8+: engineering challenges

  1. Gingerbread house engineers: build a house from biscuits and icing (or card and tape) and test how much weight the roof can hold.
  2. Lemon battery fairy light: connect several lemons in series and see if they can light an LED. See Lemon Battery.
  3. Christmas night stargazing: head out around Christmas to look at the moon and winter stars, and record them on the Stargazing Log Card. More tips in our stargazing guide.
  4. Christmas kindness: write cards for cleaners and security guards, sort old toys to donate, and record each act on the 21-Day Kindness Calendar.

Christmas outings: light displays are science too

Hong Kong puts up plenty of Christmas lights every year. When you go to see them, ask: “Which colours mix to make that light?” “Why do some lights flash?” Then try Colour Mixing Magic at home to keep the curiosity going.

FAQ

Do these activities need lots of materials?

No. Most use paper, paper cups, plastic bottles, ice and raisins. The light-up card needs an LED and a coin cell battery, available from stationery or electronics shops.

How long should we play each day over Christmas?

One 10–30 minute activity a day is plenty. Pair it with the advent calendar so children have something small to look forward to each day.

What should we watch for with young children?

Stay with them the whole time, keep small parts, batteries and hot water out of reach, and tidy up together afterwards.
