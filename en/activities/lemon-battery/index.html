🍋 Lemon Battery

Ages 9–12 | About 30 min | Indoors | Science

🧰 You'll need

  • 3–4 lemons
  • 3–4 galvanised (zinc-coated) nails
  • 3–4 copper strips or thick copper wire
  • Crocodile clip wires
  • A small LED

🧭 How to play

  1. Roll each lemon gently on the table to release more juice.
  2. Push one nail and one copper strip into each lemon, not touching each other.
  3. Connect the copper of one lemon to the nail of the next to join them in series.
  4. Connect the two free ends to the LED and see if it lights (LEDs have a + and − side, so swap the wires if it doesn't).
🎓 What they learn: Chemical energy turns into electrical energy, and introduces series circuits and positive and negative terminals.

💬 Questions to ask

  • Is one lemon enough?
  • What happens with potatoes or oranges?
  • Why do the lemons need to be linked one after another?

🚀 Extension challenge

Use a multimeter to measure the voltage of one, two and three lemons and record the change.

⚠️ Safety: Don't eat the lemons afterwards, and take care with the sharp nails. Always do activities with an adult.
