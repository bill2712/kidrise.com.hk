About this kit

About the Little Engineer Kit

Engineers don't get it right first time. They design, test and improve. Every mission in the Little Engineer Kit is an open challenge with no single answer, only better designs. Using lolly sticks, rubber bands, wires and LEDs, children explore structures, levers, circuits and friction, and learn to compare their designs with data.

  • Engineering challenges have no standard answer, which is the best way to build problem solving and creativity
  • Every mission involves measuring and recording data, so maths practice comes naturally
  • Failure is part of design, which builds a growth mindset

Mission 1

Paper Bridge Challenge

⏱ 30 min📍 Indoors

Goal: Explore how shape affects strength, and practise fair testing and recording data.

🧰 You'll need

  • Engineering notebook
  • (From home) A4 paper, two stacks of books, coins, tape

🧭 Steps

  1. Put two stacks of books 15 cm apart, lay a flat sheet across as a bridge and count how many coins it holds.
  2. Fold the paper into a U-shape, a zigzag or a tube, and test again.
  3. Test each design twice and record the average.
  4. Find the strongest design and think about why.
  5. Challenge: hold as many coins as possible with just one sheet.

💬 Questions to ask

  • “Why is folded paper so much stronger?”
  • “Which real bridges use similar shapes?”
  • “What makes a test fair?”

🚀 Extension challenge

Move the books further apart. Is your strongest design still the strongest?

🎯 Try it: Paper Bridge Challenge
2
Mission 2

Lever Launcher

⏱ 40 min📍 Indoors

Goal: Learn about levers and pivots, and how the pivot's position changes how far things fly.

🧰 You'll need

  • Lolly sticks
  • Rubber bands
  • Bottle cap
  • Tape measure
  • (From home) paper balls or cotton wool balls

🧭 Steps

  1. Follow the mission card to band the lolly sticks into a catapult, with the bottle cap as the launch cup.
  2. Stick a strip of tape on the floor as the start line.
  3. Launch a paper ball 3 times, measuring and recording each distance.
  4. Move the pivot (the crosswise stick) forward or back and test 3 more times.
  5. Compare the data to find the pivot position that launches furthest.

💬 Questions to ask

  • “Does it go further with the pivot near the cup or far from it?”
  • “Does pressing harder always launch further?”
  • “How is a seesaw like a catapult?”

🚀 Extension challenge

Make a target, work out your hit rate, then improve your aim.

🎯 Try it: Lolly Stick Catapult
3
Mission 3

Light It Up

⏱ 30 min📍 Indoors

Goal: Learn what makes a simple circuit, and tell conductors from insulators.

🧰 You'll need

  • Crocodile clip wires
  • LED
  • AA battery box (needs 2 AA batteries)
  • (From home) spoon, eraser, pencil lead, foil, wooden peg, etc.

🧭 Steps

  1. Follow the mission card to connect the battery box, wires and LED in a loop so the LED lights up.
  2. Open one clip and watch the LED go out: a circuit must be closed for electricity to flow.
  3. Leave a gap in the circuit and test different objects in it one by one.
  4. Record which objects light the LED (conductors) and which don't (insulators).
  5. Draw a diagram of your circuit.

💬 Questions to ask

  • “Does the LED light if you connect it the other way round?”
  • “Why are wires covered in plastic?”
  • “Which appliances at home use switches? How does a switch work?”

🚀 Extension challenge

Connect two LEDs side by side (parallel) or one after the other (series) and compare the brightness.

🎯 Try it: Lemon Battery
4
Mission 4

Ramp Racer

⏱ 35 min📍 Indoors

Goal: Explore how steepness and surfaces change how far a car rolls, and learn about friction.

🧰 You'll need

  • Mini car wheel and axle set
  • Tape measure and angle card
  • (From home) stiff card, books, a towel, foil

🧭 Steps

  1. Build the car and make a ramp from card and books.
  2. With the ramp on 1 book, test 3 times and measure how far the car rolls.
  3. Raise it to 2 and then 3 books and repeat.
  4. Keep the height the same, cover the floor at the bottom with a towel or foil, and test again.
  5. Compare the data: which condition makes the car go furthest?

💬 Questions to ask

  • “Does a higher ramp always make the car go further?”
  • “Which has more friction, the towel or the foil?”
  • “Why should we walk slowly down a slope on a wet day?”

🚀 Extension challenge

Improve the car (add weight, change wheels) and try to break the distance record.

🎯 Try it: Toy Parachute
5
Mission 5

Earthquake Tower

⏱ 45 min📍 Indoors

Goal: Put structural knowledge together and work through the whole engineering design cycle.

🧰 You'll need

  • Lolly sticks and wooden pegs
  • Engineering notebook
  • (From home) a baking tray, paper cups, tape, a timer

🧭 Steps

  1. The challenge: build a tower at least 30 cm tall with 20 lolly sticks and 10 pegs.
  2. Draw a design first and predict its weak points.
  3. Put the tower on the baking tray and shake it gently side to side for 10 seconds to simulate an earthquake.
  4. Record whether it fell and which part came loose first.
  5. Improve the design and test again, for at least two rounds of improvements.

💬 Questions to ask

  • “Which is steadier, a wide base or a narrow one?”
  • “Which is stronger, a triangle or a square?”
  • “How do real skyscrapers survive earthquakes?”

🚀 Extension challenge

Add a load: the top must hold a paper cup with 10 coins in it.

5-week plan

5 weekends,
one mission a week

No need to do them all at once. Pick a time each week, and add a completion stamp to the mission passport when you finish.

Tips for parents

  • Let children try first, and resist giving them the “right” design too soon
  • Record data in every mission to build the habit of drawing conclusions from evidence
  • After a collapse, ask “where did it go wrong?” before improving it together
  • Photograph every design to make an engineering portfolio
⚠️ Safety: Circuit missions use AA batteries only. Never connect to mains power or open batteries. The catapult may only launch paper balls or cotton wool balls, never aimed at people or animals. Rubber bands and small parts are not suitable for children under 3.

FAQ

Little Engineer Kit FAQ

When will the Little Engineer Kit launch?

It's in development and play testing now. Join the waitlist and we'll tell you first.

Is the circuit mission safe?

The circuit only uses AA batteries and LEDs, which are low voltage. The mission card sets out clear safety rules and we recommend an adult is present. Batteries are not included.

Can a 6-year-old do it?

Ages 6–7 can complete it with an adult's help, focusing on building and testing. From age 8, children can record data and improve designs on their own.

Can schools use it?

Yes. We also make school sets designed for classes of 20–30. See our Schools page or contact us for a quote.
