About this kit

About the Light & Shadow Kit

Light is all around children every day, but few stop to ask: why do shadows grow and shrink? How do mirrors bend light? Is there really a rainbow hidden in white light? With a torch, mirrors and a prism, the Light & Shadow Kit lets children complete 5 light missions in the living room, ending with a kaleidoscope they build themselves.

  • Light and shadow is a key primary science topic, and one of the easiest to explore safely at home
  • Most missions are indoors, so they work on rainy days and in the evening
  • It mixes crafts and art, so even children who “don't like science” are drawn in

5 missions

5 missions, every step shared

Each mission comes with materials, steps, questions for parents and an extension challenge. No kit yet? You can try most missions with things you have at home.

1
Mission 1

Shadow Theatre

⏱ 30 min📍 Indoors (dim room)

Goal: Understand that shadows form where light is blocked, and explore how distance changes a shadow's size.

🧰 You'll need

  • LED torch
  • Shadow theatre frame and screen
  • Shadow puppet templates
  • (From home) scissors, skewers or straws

🧭 Steps

  1. Cut out 2–3 shadow puppets and tape them to skewers.
  2. Turn off the lights and shine the torch at the screen from behind the theatre.
  3. Hold a puppet between the torch and the screen and look at the shadow.
  4. Move the puppet slowly towards the torch, then towards the screen, and watch the shadow change size.
  5. Make up a 1-minute story with the puppets and perform it for the family.

💬 Questions to ask

  • “When the puppet is close to the torch, does the shadow get bigger or smaller? Why?”
  • “Can you make two shadows? (Hint: two torches)”
  • “Do shadows have colours?”

🚀 Extension challenge

In the daytime, go outside and track how the sun's shadow changes over a day.

Mission 2

Can Light Get Through?

⏱ 25 min📍 Indoors

Goal: Sort materials into transparent, translucent and opaque, practising prediction and classification.

🧰 You'll need

  • LED torch
  • Coloured filters
  • (From home) cling film, baking paper, tissue, foil, plastic bag, fabric, card, etc.

🧭 Steps

  1. Collect 8 materials from home and ask your child to predict whether light will get through each one.
  2. In a dark spot, shine the torch through each material and see how much light reaches the wall.
  3. Sort the results: transparent (most light gets through), translucent (some gets through), opaque (none gets through).
  4. Compare the predictions with the results. How many were right?
  5. Talk about it: what kind of material is used for curtains, sunglasses and lampshades?

💬 Questions to ask

  • “Why do bathroom windows use frosted glass?”
  • “What happens if you stack two translucent sheets?”
  • “Is any material sometimes see-through and sometimes not?”

🚀 Extension challenge

Layer the coloured filters. What do red + yellow and yellow + blue make?

3
Mission 3

Mirror Challenge

⏱ 30 min📍 Indoors

Goal: Learn about reflection by angling a mirror to make light reach a target.

🧰 You'll need

  • LED torch
  • Safety mirrors
  • (From home) paper cups, tape, a target card

🧭 Steps

  1. Stick a target card on the wall or the edge of a table (for example, draw some treasure).
  2. The torch can't point at the target directly, only at the mirror.
  3. Angle the mirror until the light bounces onto the target.
  4. Level up: use two mirrors so the light turns twice before it reaches the target.
  5. Draw the path of the light and mark where the mirrors are.

💬 Questions to ask

  • “When you turn the mirror, how far does the spot of light move?”
  • “How does a periscope use mirrors to see over things?”
  • “Where do we use reflection in everyday life?”

🚀 Extension challenge

Make a mirror-symmetry drawing, or build a wooden periscope model.

4
Mission 4

Rainbow Maker

⏱ 20 min📍 Indoors (sunny window)

Goal: Discover that white light splits into many colours.

🧰 You'll need

  • Acrylic prism
  • (From home) white paper, a glass of water, crayons

🧭 Steps

  1. Put a sheet of white paper by a sunny window.
  2. Hold the prism in the sunlight and turn it slowly until a rainbow appears on the paper.
  3. Count the colours and draw them in order.
  4. Swap the prism for a glass of water by the window and look for a rainbow on the floor.
  5. Compare the two rainbows.

💬 Questions to ask

  • “Are the rainbow's colours always in the same order?”
  • “Why do we sometimes see a rainbow after rain?”
  • “Can torchlight make a rainbow? Try it!”

🚀 Extension challenge

Record when you see rainbows by the window over a month and look for a pattern.

🎯 Try it: Colour Mixing Magic
5
Mission 5

Build a Kaleidoscope

⏱ 40 min📍 Indoors

Goal: Use reflection to build a kaleidoscope and explore repeating, symmetrical patterns.

🧰 You'll need

  • Safety mirrors × 3
  • Kaleidoscope tube and beads
  • (From home) tape, baking paper

🧭 Steps

  1. Tape the three mirrors into a triangular prism with the mirror sides facing in.
  2. Slide it into the tube, cover one end with baking paper and add the beads.
  3. Leave a small hole at the other end to look through.
  4. Point it at the light, turn it and watch the patterns change.
  5. Draw your three favourite patterns and count how many times each repeats.

💬 Questions to ask

  • “Why does one bead turn into lots of beads?”
  • “How would the pattern change with only two mirrors?”
  • “Is the pattern symmetrical? Where are the lines of symmetry?”

🚀 Extension challenge

Replace the beads with other materials, such as scraps of coloured paper or small stickers, and compare.

5-week plan

5 weekends,
one mission a week

No need to do them all at once. Pick a time each week, and add a completion stamp to the mission passport when you finish.

Tips for parents

  • Shadow and reflection missions work best in a darker room, so after dinner is ideal
  • Never shine the torch into eyes, and never let children look at the sun
  • Encourage children to predict before testing and to note what they got right and wrong
  • After the kaleidoscope, invite your child to design their own shadow puppet story
⚠️ Safety: Never look at the sun or reflect sunlight into eyes with mirrors or the prism. Never shine the torch into eyes. Use scissors with an adult. Small beads are not suitable for children under 3; put them away after play.

FAQ

Light & Shadow Kit FAQ

When will the Light & Shadow Kit launch?

It's in development and play testing now. Join the waitlist and we'll tell you first, and invite some families to try it before launch.

Do we need sunshine to play?

Most missions only need the torch, so they work at night and on rainy days. The Rainbow Maker works best in sunlight, but you can try it with the torch.

Is it too hard for a 5-year-old?

For ages 5–6, focus on the shadow theatre, the light test and rainbows with an adult's help. We suggest building the kaleidoscope together.

Are the mirrors safe?

The mirrors are plastic with rounded corners. Before launch we'll confirm the applicable standards and compliance documents under Hong Kong's toy safety rules.
