About this kit

About the Nature Explorer Kit

The Nature Explorer Kit is Kidrise's first themed kit. Armed with a magnifier and an observation journal, children start in the park downstairs, getting to know plants, insects and sounds, then turn what they collect into “My Nature Museum”. No long hikes needed: any park or podium garden in Hong Kong will do.

  • Hong Kong children spend most of their time indoors. This kit gives them a reason to go outside
  • Observing, sorting, measuring and recording are the foundations of all science learning
  • Every mission ends with a clear result, so children feel successful and parents can see the learning

Mission 1

Leaf Detective

⏱ 30 min📍 Park + indoors

Goal: Learn to notice details and sort leaves using rules you make up yourself.

🧰 You'll need

  • Collection bag
  • Rubbing crayons and paper
  • Observation journal
  • (From home) a pencil

🧭 Steps

  1. Collect 8–10 leaves that have already fallen in the park (don't pick leaves from trees).
  2. At home, lay the leaves out and look at their edges and veins with the magnifier.
  3. Ask your child to sort the leaves into two groups and explain the rule, such as big and small, or smooth and jagged.
  4. Try another way of sorting. How many ways can the same pile be grouped?
  5. Pick the two most special leaves, make crayon rubbings of the veins and stick them in the journal.

💬 Questions to ask

  • “How did you sort them? Why that way?”
  • “Is there a third way to sort them?”
  • “What do the veins look like? Branches? Rivers?”

🚀 Extension challenge

Are all the leaves on one tree the same? Next time, collect 5 leaves from a single tree and compare.

2
Mission 2

Tiny Universe

⏱ 30 min📍 Park

Goal: Use a tool to look closer and complete a first observation report.

🧰 You'll need

  • Magnifier
  • Clear bug viewer
  • Observation journal

🧭 Steps

  1. In the park, choose a small patch about 30 × 30 cm, such as a corner of grass or the edge of a flower bed.
  2. Look slowly with the magnifier: soil, petals, bark, stones. Does any small creature pass by?
  3. Choose the most interesting thing and draw it in the circle on the report page.
  4. Describe it with three words, such as “rough, brown, grooved”.
  5. If you find a small insect, put it in the viewer for a short look, then let it go where you found it.

💬 Questions to ask

  • “What's different when you look with the magnifier?”
  • “How many kinds of living things live in this little patch?”
  • “If you were an ant, would this place feel huge?”

🚀 Extension challenge

Come back to the same spot a week later and see what has changed.

🎯 Try it: The World Under a Magnifier
3
Mission 3

Seed Diary

⏱ 5–10 min a day × 7–14 days📍 Indoors

Goal: Learn what plants need to grow, and practise daily recording and fair comparison.

🧰 You'll need

  • Seed pack
  • Growing cups × 2 and cotton wool
  • Observation journal
  • (From home) water and a ruler

🧭 Steps

  1. Line both cups with damp cotton wool and put 3 seeds in each.
  2. Put one cup by a window and the other in a cupboard (no light), keeping everything else the same.
  3. Ask your child to predict which cup will sprout first.
  4. Observe at the same time each day, measure the shoots and record them in the diary.
  5. On day 7, compare the two cups and talk about how light affects plants.

💬 Questions to ask

  • “Why do both cups need the same amount of water?”
  • “What colour are the shoots in the cupboard? Why?”
  • “What's inside a seed that lets it sprout?”

🚀 Extension challenge

Change a different condition and try again: water vs no water, warm vs cold.

🎯 Try it: Seed Sprouting Diary
4
Mission 4

Sound Map

⏱ 20 min📍 Park

Goal: Observe the environment by listening, and tell natural sounds from man-made ones.

🧰 You'll need

  • Observation journal (sound map page)
  • (From home) a pencil and a timer

🧭 Steps

  1. Find a safe spot to sit and draw “me” in the middle of the map.
  2. Close your eyes and listen quietly for 1 minute.
  3. Open your eyes and draw each sound on the map by direction and distance.
  4. Use different symbols for loud and quiet, natural and man-made sounds.
  5. Count them up: are there more natural sounds or man-made ones?

💬 Questions to ask

  • “Which sound was furthest away? How do you know?”
  • “How would the map be different in a country park?”
  • “Did you hear a sound you've never noticed before?”

🚀 Extension challenge

Make a map at the same spot in the morning and again in the evening, and compare.

🎯 Try it: Sound Bottle Matching
5
Mission 5

My Nature Museum

⏱ 40 min📍 Indoors

Goal: Organise and display the collection, practising sorting, naming and speaking.

🧰 You'll need

  • Specimen cards and bags
  • Leaves, seeds and records from earlier missions
  • Completion stickers

🧭 Steps

  1. Bring out everything collected and recorded in the earlier missions.
  2. Fill in a specimen card for each item: name (you can invent one), place, date and features.
  3. Sort the specimens into 2–3 display areas using your own rules.
  4. Set up a small family exhibition in a box or on a shelf.
  5. Invite the family to visit, with your child as the guide presenting their three favourite exhibits.

💬 Questions to ask

  • “How will you divide up the display areas?”
  • “Which exhibit was hardest to find? How did you find it?”
  • “If you added a new area, what would you collect?”

🚀 Extension challenge

Photograph the exhibition and make “My Nature Guide”, adding new pages after every outing.

5-week plan

5 weekends,
one mission a week

No need to do them all at once. Pick a time each week, and add a completion stamp to the mission passport when you finish.

Tips for parents

  • Do one mission at a time. There's no need to finish everything in one weekend
  • Let your child carry the magnifier and journal so they feel in charge
  • Questions and observation matter most. Wrong answers are fine
  • Check the weather before you go and bring water, a hat and insect repellent
⚠️ Safety: Outdoor missions need an adult. Only collect fallen leaves; don't pick plants or catch insects. Return any small creatures in the bug viewer quickly. Wash hands afterwards. Seeds are not for eating.

FAQ

Nature Explorer Kit FAQ

We live in the city without a big park. Can we still play?

Yes. Podium gardens, sitting-out areas, flower beds and even balcony pots work for most missions. The sound map works anywhere outdoors.

Can a 4-year-old do it alone?

For ages 4–5 we recommend a parent throughout, reading the cards while the child does the observing. From age 6, children can follow most steps on the cards themselves.

What if the seeds don't sprout?

Check the cotton wool is damp (but not soaking) and that it isn't too cold. Sprouting takes time, and “it didn't sprout” is a result worth investigating too.

Can the tools be reused?

Yes. The magnifier, bug viewer, ruler and bags are all reusable, and extra journal pages can be printed free from our Chinese site.
